The basics

What is extra virgin olive oil?

Extra virgin olive oil is the juice of the olive, obtained solely by mechanical means: the olives are washed, crushed, malaxed and separated – without solvents and without refining. It is the highest grade defined by EU law.

To be called “extra virgin”, an oil must meet precise chemical limits and pass the judgement of a tasting panel: it needs a perceptible fruitiness and no defects at all.

The heart of quality

Polyphenols: the strength of the oil

Polyphenols are natural substances the olive tree produces to protect its fruit. Extra virgin olive oil contains dozens of them; the best known are hydroxytyrosol, tyrosol, oleuropein (in its derived forms), oleocanthal and oleacein.

They give the oil its bitter and pungent notes: the peppery tickle in the throat after tasting is mainly due to oleocanthal. A polyphenol-rich oil is also more stable – it oxidises more slowly and keeps its aromas for longer.

The amount depends on the variety, ripeness, climate and above all on processing. That is why we harvest early and process the olives within a few hours in our own mill, just steps from the grove.

What science says – and what the law allows

Authorised health claim (Regulation (EU) No 432/2012): “Olive oil polyphenols contribute to the protection of blood lipids from oxidative stress.” The beneficial effect is obtained with a daily intake of 20 g of olive oil.

Extra virgin olive oil is a cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet. It consists mostly of oleic acid, a monounsaturated fatty acid, and naturally contains vitamin E (tocopherols), squalene and pigments such as chlorophylls and carotenoids.

How we work

Organic, from tree to oil

  • Organic farming under Regulation (EU) 2018/848: no chemical herbicides, no synthetic fertilisers; ground cover, green manure and careful pruning.
  • Control body: Q Certificazioni S.r.l. – code IT-BIO-014, origin: Agricoltura Italia.
  • Early harvest between October and early November, as the olives begin to change colour.
  • By hand or with harvesting rakes, onto nets: no olive is picked up from the ground.
  • Milling within a few hours in our own estate mill, with cold extraction (below 27 °C): from harvest to bottle, everything on our estate.
  • Storage in stainless steel tanks under inert gas, protected from light and heat.

Tasting

How to recognise a great oil

Fruity

Aromas of fresh olive, cut grass, artichoke, green almond, tomato. A “green” fruitiness reveals an early harvest.

Bitter

A bitter note on the tongue is a mark of quality, not a defect: it shows healthy, polyphenol-rich olives.

Pungent

The peppery tickle in the throat, sometimes with a little cough, is the sign of oleocanthal. The more intense, the younger and livelier the oil.

Storage: away from light and heat, at 14–18 °C, tightly closed. Olive oil does not improve with age: it is best enjoyed within 12–18 months of milling.

In the kitchen: raw on toasted bread, pulses, vegetables and Tuscan soups – but also for cooking, as its antioxidants help extra virgin olive oil withstand heat well.
